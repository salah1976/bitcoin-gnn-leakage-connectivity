\paragraph{Elliptic++ as a complementary regime.}
Elliptic++ has $203{,}769$ transactions, of which $46{,}564$ are labelled ($4{,}545$ illicit). We train on time steps 1--29, validate on 30--34 and test on 35--49 ($16{,}670$ labelled test transactions, $1{,}083$ illicit, $6.50\%$). Money-flow edges are intra-time-step, so no edge joins the training and test periods. Using the public address--transaction files, we compute the same quantities as on BitFraud. The connectivity $\lambda$ is high ($98.1\%$ of labelled test transactions share an address with another test transaction; $74.4\%$ on the transaction graph restricted to labelled nodes), but the historical exposure of fraud is almost null: only $1.6\%$ of illicit test transactions share an address with the training period, against $37.3\%$ of licit ones. The address-history rule is at chance level (AUC-ROC $0.501$, AUC-PR $0.067$ for a prevalence of $0.065$).

Table~\ref{tab:elliptic_multiseed} reports six seeds, with each trained model evaluated under both graphs. The graph brings no gain: the GraphSAGE model with graph reaches $0.712\pm0.045$ AUC-PR, below the MLP ($0.739\pm0.008$), and removing test-to-test edges brings it to the level of the MLP ($0.738\pm0.009$). The paired difference ($+0.025\pm0.043$, five seeds out of six) is not significant and is driven by one seed. Removing history--test edges changes nothing, since none exists. Elliptic++ is thus a regime with high $\lambda$ but negligible $\rho$ for fraud, where connectivity removal has no historical signal to destroy. This is consistent with the exposure account and does not contradict it. Threshold-dependent scores such as F1 vary widely across seeds (standard deviation up to $0.11$) and are not reported.
